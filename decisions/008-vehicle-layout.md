# 008 — Compact Cab + Bed Layout

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-04-13

## Context
Sizing the chassis revealed a tension between C-130 max width (3.12m) and urban street widths (2.7-3.4m). A full-width AFV fills a residential lane. The vehicle needed to fit comfortably in urban environments alongside its open-terrain role.

## Decision
Compact cab + bed layout:
- 2-person cab (side-by-side, identical stations)
- Bed area for weapon module mounting
- Built from the ground up with armor, active protection, and NBC

## Rationale
- Urban agility — fits on streets where larger AFVs can't maneuver
- Cab + bed layout naturally separates crew from weapon module
- Keeps the vehicle well under C-130 limits — potential to carry multiple per aircraft
- Purpose-built means no compromises from adapting a civilian chassis

## Alternatives Considered
- Crew cab (4 seats + bed) — larger footprint, more crew capacity, but packaging harder
- Tandem seating — narrower but longer, unusual crew workflow
- Conventional AFV layout — too wide for urban environments

## Consequences
- Bed dimensions constrain weapon module size and mounting
- Engine packaging must work in a compact front or rear layout
- Two per C-130 may be possible — doubles strategic deployment rate
